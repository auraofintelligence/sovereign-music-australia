The Terms Table

What does it cost? What happens to your music when you stop paying? Who can train on your work? This table gathers the answers in one place, with the source noted against each fact.

Under every service

The floor everyone stands on.

On the biggest streaming service, a track needs more than 1,000 plays in a year, call it three plays a day, every day, plus a minimum number of distinct listeners, before recording royalties (the money paid for the recording itself) switch on at all. In 2024, roughly seven in eight tracks fell under that line. Picture a shelf of eight singles: seven of them earned the recording side nothing there that year.

Direct sales (selling a download or a disc straight to a listener, from your own page) have no such floor. One sale pays from the first sale. That difference matters more the smaller your audience is, which is most audiences.

The standard to ask for

What good terms look like.

Four tests, each one answerable from a service's own pages before you sign.

Test 01

Your catalogue survives your subscription

Stopping payment ends the service, not your presence in stores. Leaving is a decision you make, not a takedown that happens to you.

Test 02

Cancelling an extra is a button

Dropping an add-on removes the add-on, nothing else. Your releases stay where they are.

Test 03

Training on your work is opt-in

No model learns from your recordings unless you switch that on yourself, and the switch is somewhere you can find it.

Test 04

Fees are on a public page

Every price, and everything that renews, listed where anyone can read it before handing over a card number.

Where this leads

Reading the fine print one maker at a time keeps you out of trouble. Changing the fine print takes numbers: small operators sitting down together at the bargaining table, and makers walking in already knowing what the terms say.
