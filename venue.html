The Stage

For venues, promoters and anyone who runs a room. Three practical things: a poster that says what the act is, show formats that put lights on midweek, and ticketing whose fees are published as one plain rate.

Billing

Billing that says what it is.

Every act in this ecosystem carries a band: the four-word scale from the Maker's Mark that says how the music is made. Handmade, Human-led, AI-led or Autonomous. Put the band on the poster and on the ticket page, next to the door price, and everyone buys with their eyes open. taking shape

For the punter

No surprises

Nobody enjoys finding out mid-set that the show is not what they thought they bought. The band on the ticket page answers the question before it gets asked.

For the act

No whispers

Acts that use AI tools get talked about either way. A band on the poster replaces the rumour with the record, and lets the work be judged as work.

For the venue

No clean-up

2026 has already seen an undisclosed booking end in public apologies, with the takings given away to make amends. A small mark at the point of sale is cheaper than that night was.

The band is not a grade and the venue does not have to police it. The act records it once with their mark; the venue prints it. How the mark works.

Programming

New nights for quiet nights.

More than 1,300 Australian venues closed for good between 2020 and late 2023, about one in four: roughly one room going dark every day for the whole stretch. A format that reliably sells a Tuesday is not a novelty; it is a reason to keep the doors open. Choose a format to see how the night runs.

The generative audio-visual show

A show where the music and the visuals are made live, by machine and human together: a performer steers, the system plays, the screen answers the sound. No two nights are the same, which is the point and the poster line.

What it needs: a decent PA, a projector or screen wall, and an act comfortable working live with their tools. What it sells: a night people cannot stream at home, because it only exists in the room.

The hybrid set

A human performer on stage, directing AI instruments live: calling the changes, singing or playing over the top, shaping the set in front of the crowd. The craft is visible, which is what a live audience actually pays for.

What it needs: one performer, their rig, a normal stage. What it sells: the same thing live music has always sold, a person taking risks in real time, with a new instrument in their hands.

The listening-room premiere

An album played front to back to a seated room, with the maker there to introduce it and the band printed on the programme. Cheap to stage, easy to run midweek, and it treats a marked release as an occasion rather than an upload.

What it needs: good speakers, dim lights, chairs. What it sells: first hearing. A record only premieres once, and the room that hosts it is part of the story.

All three formats book through the same door as any other act: the maker's path shows how acts get marked and listed.

Ticketing

Ticketing without the bite.

A booking fee (the extra charge added on top of the ticket price) does not have to eat the door. These operators publish their fees as one plain rate, so the cost is known before the on-sale, not after it. This is not a list of everyone; it is the operators whose rates are published in the open. No comment on anyone else: just the numbers that exist.

Not-for-profit

Humanitix

4% + A$0.99 a ticket, plus GST

A not-for-profit ticketing operator. The profit from its booking fees goes to children's charities, so the fee line on the ticket does some good on its way through.

Independent · Australian

Oztix

around 2% + A$1 a ticket

An independent Australian operator. That rate applies to tickets under A$50 through its community arm, built for exactly the kind of small room this page is about.

Flat rate

TryBooking

2.5% + 50c a ticket

A simple published rate, low enough that a A$20 ticket stays close to A$20. Well suited to listening rooms, club nights and community shows.

Rates as published by each operator; confirm the current figures on their sites before you set your on-sale.

Licensing

The licences you already hold still apply.

Whatever the format, the usual venue music licences do not change. If your room is licensed through OneMusic Australia (the one-stop body venues license recorded and live music through), that arrangement covers your nights the same way it always has. Nothing on this page replaces it, shortcuts it or changes what you owe.

If a new format raises a question your current licence class does not obviously answer, ask OneMusic directly; that is what the one-stop door is for.
