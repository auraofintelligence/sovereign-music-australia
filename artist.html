Path 01 · For makers

You make music.

However you make it: voice and guitar, a bedroom studio, a prompt and a hundred careful takes, or all three. This page walks the road from finished song to paid maker, one step at a time, and nobody along it grades your method.

First, how do you work?

Pick the door closest to you.

The welcome changes; the road does not. All three doors lead to the same eight steps below.

Voice, hands, instruments, a room. The mark records that your work is handmade, in your own words, and the listeners who look for exactly that can find you by it. The steps below fit around how you already work; none of them ask you to change it.

A person drives; tools help where you point them. That is how a great deal of music gets made this decade, and this path treats it as ordinary, because it is. The mark lets you say which parts came from where, once, and be done with the question.

The tools generate; you direct, select and shape. Directing well takes taste, patience and a lot of discarded takes, and this path gives that work a name and a place to stand. Say what it is on the mark, and the steps below read the same for you as for anyone.

The maker's road

Eight steps, in the order they help.

Mark it

Fill in the Maker's Mark once: how the work was made, in your own words. It answers the AI questions every service now asks, from streaming credit fields to chart entry forms, so you write the facts down one time instead of five.

Release it, direct first

Sell album packs and downloads from your own page and keep the margin (the part of each sale left after costs). A worked pricing ladder from the community: A$20 for one album, A$35 for two, A$45 for three. The streaming services can come second; your own door opens first.

Choose your distributor

A distributor (the service that puts your song on Spotify and Apple Music) is a contract as well as a button. The Terms Table lays the fine print side by side, with sources linked: what it costs, what happens when you stop paying, who can train on your work. Plans start around A$34.90 a year (US$24.99, converted at A$1 = US$0.7162, 28 August 2026).

Chart it

Enter the People's Chart, where listeners vote and every entry wears its mark. The method is published and the rules are the same for every maker, whichever door you came through.

Stage it

Play live with billing that says what the act is, so punters know what they are buying before they buy. Ticketing through fair-fee operators runs around 4% plus A$0.99 a ticket: on a A$30 ticket that is about A$2.19 in fees, and the rest stays at the door.

Know where you stand

Am I allowed to sell this? What happens to my songs if I stop paying? The Rights Engine gives plain-language answers with the exact rules quoted: information, never legal advice. Registering with collecting societies (the bodies that gather royalties when radio stations and venues play your songs) is a choice with its own timing, and the choice stays yours.

Team up

Small operators can negotiate together at the bargaining table. A union for gigs exists too: Musicians Australia, about A$196.57 a year including public liability insurance (cover if someone is hurt at your show). That works out under four dollars a week.

Share the upside

Pooled channels with the split rules published, so a share of a bigger audience flows back to the makers in it. The Pool is where that lives. taking shape

The enabler, not the product

The paperwork on this road does not need your evenings. The release forms, the album-pack page and the first legal question can all be drafted by your own AI assistant working from the mark's data, ready for you to check and sign. Your hours go to the music.
