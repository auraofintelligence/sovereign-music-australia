Four barriers, coming down

Cost. Process. Time. Worry.

Every musician meets the same four walls. Each one can be lowered with tools that already exist. That is the whole project, in one line.

Cost

Releasing an album should not need a label advance. Direct-to-fan sales, fair-fee ticketing and side-by-side pricing on every service, in Australian dollars.

lower it
Process

One mark on your work that answers every form: the streaming services, the charts, the poster on the wall. Fill it in once, use it everywhere.

lower it
Time

AI is the enabler here, not the product: it drafts the paperwork, preps the release pack and answers the first legal question, so the music gets the hours.

lower it
Worry

Am I allowed to sell this? What happens if I stop paying my distributor? Clear answers with the exact rules quoted, so decisions stop being guesses.

Choose your path

This place reads differently depending on who you are.

Pick a door. Each path walks you through the ecosystem from your side of it, one step at a time, with choices along the way.

Path 01

You make music

However you make it: voice and guitar, a bedroom studio, a prompt and a hundred takes, or all three. Mark it, release it, chart it, play it, get paid for it.

 Path 02

You love music

Set your own dial for what you want to hear, vote in a chart where your vote is the chart, and put money straight into the hands that made the song.

 Path 03

You run a stage

New show formats for quiet nights, billing that tells punters exactly what they are buying, and ticketing that does not eat the door.

 Path 04

You build the future

Toolmakers, organisations and policy people: the standards to adopt, the data to share and the public processes where this ecosystem shows up.

The whole machine

Eight layers, approached all at once.

Not a pilot and a promise: a complete ecosystem where each layer feeds the others. The mark feeds the chart, the chart feeds the awards, the rights engine backs the bargaining table, and the pool pays the people. See how it fits together.

Layer 01

The Maker's Mark

A hallmark for music: how a work was made, recorded once, readable everywhere. Gold has carried maker's marks for centuries; songs can too.

 Layer 02

The People's Chart

A chart where listeners vote and every entry wears its mark. Open method, published rules, no bots.

 Layer 03

The Auric Awards

Recognition for the craft as it actually happens now, including the craft of directing AI well.

 Layer 04

The Rights Engine

Plain-language answers about music and the law, with the exact rules quoted and cited. Information, never legal advice.

 Layer 05

The Bargaining Table

Small operators teaming up to negotiate with big platforms, using a route the competition regulator already provides.

 Layer 06

The Terms Table

Every distributor's fine print, side by side, with sources linked: what it costs, what happens when you leave, who can train on your work.

 Layer 07

The Stage

Live formats for the AI era, billing that says what the act is, and fair-fee ticketing for member shows.

 Layer 08

The Pool

Shared channels and shared revenue with the split rules published, on the co-operative model that already works elsewhere.

Open to every way of making

This ecosystem is not an AI club. A song made with two hands and a guitar belongs here exactly as much as a song made with a prompt and a hundred careful choices. The mark simply says how a thing was made; the listener decides what that means to them. Nobody here grades your method. The door is the same width for everyone.
